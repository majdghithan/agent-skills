# Maghrebi Arabic (دارجة) - writing guide

**Coverage:** Moroccan Darija (Casablanca / Rabat urban) with Algerian (Algiers) and Tunisian (Tunis) columns. Every form below is cited to the sources at the end; anything the sources didn't settle is listed in the last section and must not be generated.


## 0. Pick ONE country. These are not interchangeable.

Mutual intelligibility is high between neighbours but **low between Moroccan and Tunisian**. [MaghrebWiki]

**False friends that break a text:**

| Form | Moroccan | Algerian | Tunisian |
|---|---|---|---|
| **باش** | "in order to" | - | **future marker** ("will") |
| **غادي** | **future marker** ("will") | unsourced | "there" (place) |
| **واش** | **yes/no question particle** (واش حابين تجيوا؟) | **"what"** | - |

So باش نكتب means "so that I write" in Morocco and "I will write" in Tunisia. Never mix columns. [Wikt-bash] [Wikt-ghadi] [Wikt-wash]

| Feature | Moroccan (default) | Algerian | Tunisian |
|---|---|---|---|
| "not" + verb | ما...ش (ما كتبش) | ما...ش (ما لعبتش) | ما...ش (ما فهمتش) |
| "not" + noun/adj | ماشي | unsourced | موش / مش |
| Future | غادي / غاد / غا (north: ها-) | رايح | باش / بش / ماش |
| Habitual present | كـ- / تـ- (كنكتب) | راني / رانا + verb | **bare verb, no prefix** |
| "there is / isn't" | كاين / ماكاينش | ماكاش | فمّة / مافمّاش |
| "what" | شنو / آش (north: شني) | واش | شنوة / آش |
| "where" | فين | وين | فين / وين |
| "now" | دابا | unsourced | توّة |
| "a lot / very" | بزاف | unsourced for Algerian | برشا |
| "of" (possession) | ديال | تاع | متاع |

Sources: [MoroccoWiki] [AlgeriaWiki] [TunisiaWiki] [Wikt-*]. Blank cells mean no source was found; ask a native speaker rather than borrowing the neighbouring column.

## 1. Negation

- **ما...ش wraps the verb** in all three: ما كتبش، ما كيكتبش (Moroccan), ما لعبتش (Algerian), ما فهمتش / ما ناحكيش (Tunisian). It wraps attached object pronouns too. [MoroccoWiki] [AlgeriaWiki] [Wikivoyage]
- **Non-verbal negation**: Moroccan **ماشي**: هو ماشي مغربي. Tunisian **موش / مش** (from ماهوش), with inflected forms مانيش، ماهومش، ماكومش. [Wikt-mashi] [Wikt-mush] [SciELO]
- **Existentials**: Moroccan كاين / كاينة / كاينين -> **ماكاينش**; Algerian **ماكاش**; Tunisian فمّة -> **مافمّاش**. Careful: Tunisian كاين is a modal ("to seem"), not the existential. [Wikt-kayn] [Souag] [Ithaca]
- **ما بغيتش** = "I don't want" (Moroccan). [DarijaSchool]
- **Moroccan negatives without ش**: walu، حتى حاجة، حتى واحد. [MoroccoWiki]
- **Not sourced:** the negative imperative (ما تمشيش), the negated future (ماغاديش), and Algerian ماشي / ماهوش. Don't generate them; see section 10.

## 2. Future

- **Moroccan غادي / غاد / غا** + verb: غانكتب. Northern Morocco (Tangier, Tetouan) uses **ها-** instead. [MoroccoWiki]
- **Moroccan ماشي also marks the future** (ماشي ناكل فهداك المطعم), but it is also the nominal negator, so **prefer غادي** in generated text to avoid ambiguity. [Wikt-mashi]
- **Algerian رايح** + verb, with a caveat from the source: Algerians often just use the present or present continuous instead of a future form. [AlgeriaWiki]
- **Tunisian باش / بش / ماش** + verb. [TunisiaWiki]

## 3. Aspect - the biggest country split

- **Moroccan كـ- / تـ- marks the habitual/indicative**: كنكتب (I write), كتكتب، كيكتب، كنكتبو (we write). كـ dominates in the north, تـ in the south, and Oujda uses no preverb at all. [MoroccoWiki]
- **Tunisian has NO indicative prefix**: the bare imperfect does the job, and there is no indicative/subjunctive distinction. **Writing كنكتب in Tunisian is a hard error.** [TunisiaWiki]
- **Algerian راني / رانا + verb** for an ongoing action: راني نكتب، رانا نكتبو. [AlgeriaWiki]
- **Tunisian قاعد + imperfect** for the progressive. [TunisiaWiki]
- **Known gap: no Tunisian or Algerian obligation form is sourced** ("I have to"). Moroccan has خاص / خاصني; the other two columns have nothing. Rebuild the sentence as intent (باش + verb) and say the obligation nuance is not covered, rather than borrowing خاصني across the border.
- **Participle ماشي** = going right now: آنا دابا ماشي للمدرسة. [Wikt-mashi]
- **Moroccan خاص / خاصني** ("I have to") takes a bare verb: خاصني نمشي للمكتبة باش ناخد داك الكتاب. [Wikt-khass]

## 4. Demonstratives, question words, relative

- **Demonstratives**: Moroccan هاد / هادي / هادو / هادشي، داك / ديك; Algerian had / hadi / hadu، dak / dik / duk; Tunisian هاذا / هاذي / هاذومة (Sfax هاكومة / هاكة / هاكي). Tunisian uses هاذا, **not** Moroccan هاد. [MoroccoWikiAr] [AlgeriaWiki] [TunisiaWiki]
- **Question words** (see the table in section 0 for the traps):
  - "what": Moroccan شنو / آش (north شني), Algerian واش, Tunisian شنوة / آش. شنو is **not** a Maghreb marker: it is also Gulf, Iraqi, and Sudanese. [Wikt-shnu]
  - "where": فين (Moroccan and Tunisian), وين (Algerian and Tunisian). [Wikt-fin] [Wikt-win]
  - "why": علاش everywhere (Algerian also علاه). "how": كيفاش. [Wikt-alash] [Wikt-kifash]
  - "when": Moroccan فوقاش (most regions), إمتى (Atlantic), فوياخ (northwest); Algerian and Tunisian وقتاش. [MoroccoWiki] [Wikt]
  - "who": شكون (Moroccan subject position only, otherwise من). "how much": شحال and قداش are **both** used in Morocco and Algeria; neither is country-exclusive. [Wikt-shkun] [Wikt-shhal] [Wikt-qaddash]
- **Relative اللي** (Tunisian إلي). [MoroccoWiki] [TunisiaWiki]
- **Not sourced:** داكشي, the position of Moroccan demonstratives relative to the noun, and the complementizer بلي. Don't assert them.

## 5. Pronouns - the Maghrebi signature

- **The n- prefix on the 1st person singular "distinguishes Maghrebi Arabic from all other varieties of Arabic".** نكتب = **I** write; نكتبو = **we** write. Moroccan كنكتب / كنكتبو, Algerian نكتب / نكتبو, Tunisian نـ / نـ...ـو. An agent that writes أكتب for "I write" has left the Maghreb. [MaghrebWiki] [MoroccoWiki] [AlgeriaWiki]
- **Tunisian pronouns** (7, with **no gender split in the 2nd person singular**): أني، إنتِي، هوة، هية، أحنا، انتوم، هومة. [TunisiaWiki]
- **Possession**: ديال (Moroccan), تاع (Algerian), متاع (Tunisian). This is a tendency, not a clean split: تاع also exists in Morocco, and Tunisian lists تاع beside متاع. [Wikt-dyal] [Wikt-taa] [Wikt-mtaa]
- **Not sourced:** the full Moroccan pronoun set, including the نتا / نتي gender split. Ask a native speaker.

## 6. Spelling habits

- **ق in Morocco** splits lexically into /q/ and /g/ in Casablanca, and stays /q/ in Rabat, Fez, and Marrakesh. There is **no glottal-stop ق** in Moroccan (that is a Cairo and Levant feature). [MoroccoWiki]
- **/g/ is written ڭ** in Moroccan (أڭادير، الڭروج); sources vary between ڭ and ݣ and call ڭ preferred. Algerian writes ق as **ڨ**. [Gaf] [AlgeriaWikiAr]
- **ما attached or separate** (مافهمتش vs ما فهمتش): both are attested and there is **no standard**. Be internally consistent; that is why normalization projects like NOTA and CODA* exist. [MANorm] [NOTA]
- **Arabizi is the normal informal script**, especially online: Moroccan uses 2 = ء، 3 = ع، 7 = ح، 9 = ق (plus 5). Tunisian Arabizi is the social-media norm (the TUNIZI dataset is 100k comments), and Algerian romanized text has its own treebank (NArabizi), with heavy French and Amazigh code-switching. **No source gives per-country script percentages, so don't quote one.** Write in Arabic script unless the user writes in Arabizi. [MoroccoWikiAr] [MANorm] [TUNIZI] [NArabizi]
- **French loans in Arabic script** are normal: تيلفازة، طونوبيل (Moroccan); طونوبيل، كوزينة، فرشيطة (Algerian). Algerian keeps French phonemes in loans. [MoroccoWiki] [AlgeriaWiki]

## 8. French and English tech terms

- **The Maghreb runs on French where the Mashreq runs on English.** Moroccan, Algerian, and Tunisian Arabic have absorbed a large French loan layer, and sentence-internal code-switching is documented in all three countries. Don't write Mashreqi-English tech copy for a Maghrebi audience by default. [Playaling] [Lund] [Albany] [PMC]
- **Borrowed tech items are overwhelmingly nouns**: one Tunisian study counted 268 loanword instances as 236 nouns, 24 verbs, 8 adjectives, with a growing list of technical terms that have no Tunisian equivalent. [Albany]
- **French verbs do take Arabic morphology in Moroccan** (loan infinitives under perfective morphology), **but no source gave an attested form**, so this guide ships the pattern without examples. Don't invent ones like تيليفونا or كونيكتا. [Academia-FrenchVerbs]
- **Which language dominates tech vocabulary per country is unsourced.** Ask the user, or keep the term in the script the brand already uses.

## 9. Worked rewrites (MSA -> Moroccan)

Grammar forms come from the sections above; ordinary content words are not individually sourced.

**1. Obligation + bare verb** (يجب, أن)
- MSA: يجب أن أراجع الـ logs قبل الـ deploy.
- Moroccan: خاصني نشوف الـ logs قبل الـ deploy.

**2. Habitual with كـ-, and the n- prefix** (أعمل)
- MSA: أكتب الـ tests كل يوم.
- Moroccan: كنكتب الـ tests كل يوم.
- نكتب alone is "I write" in the Maghreb; كنكتب is the habitual.

**3. Existential negation** (لا يوجد)
- MSA: لا يوجد أي خطأ في الـ log.
- Moroccan: ماكاينش أي error فالـ log.
- Algerian: ماكاش أي error. Tunisian: مافمّاش أي error.

**4. Future + "now"** (سوف, الآن) and the country trap
- MSA: سوف أكتب التحديث الآن.
- Moroccan: غادي نكتب الـ update دابا.
- Tunisian: باش نكتب الـ update توّة.
- Writing باش in the Moroccan line would turn "I will write" into "so that I write".

## 10. Deliberately NOT included (unverified)

Don't generate these, they are not attested in the sources checked: the negative imperative (ما تمشيش) and negated future (ماغاديش); Algerian ماشي / ماهوش, غادي, حـ, دروك ("now"), بزاف, and دار; Moroccan تنـ as a northern/Fessi form (the sources actually contradict this); a Moroccan لا- preverb; the participles جاي / واكل; a bare imperfect after بغيت / لازم / نقدر; داكشي and demonstrative position; the full Moroccan pronoun set including نتا / نتي; بلي as a complementizer; ژ; أيضاً -> زعما or حتى (زعما actually means "supposedly"); يستطيع -> نقدر; فقط -> غير or بس; Tunisian حب for "want" (only partly attested); and anything Libyan **as Maghrebi** (Libyan has no column here; for a Libyan request see the uncovered-dialect rule in SKILL.md).
